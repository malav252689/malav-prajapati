Research Paper – Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Lid-Driven Cavity Flow

This research examines the capability of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to serve as mesh-free and data-efficient alternatives to conventional Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) solvers by solving the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations for the classical lid-driven cavity problem. PINNs embed the governing physical laws directly into the neural network loss function, enabling the model to satisfy continuity, momentum conservation, and boundary conditions without relying on numerical discretization or large CFD datasets. This makes them particularly attractive for problems where mesh generation is computationally intensive or where data is sparse. The lid-driven cavity flow was chosen as the benchmark because of its simple geometry and complex vortical features, including primary vortices, corner eddies, and sharp gradients, making it an ideal test case to evaluate accuracy and stability in physics-guided learning.

The study was implemented using NVIDIA’s PhysicsNeMo framework, which provides symbolic PDE definitions, automatic differentiation for computing residuals, and a variety of neural architectures designed for physics-constrained learning. The cavity geometry and boundary conditions were encoded symbolically, and training points were sampled throughout the domain and along the boundaries. Across this setup, several PINN architectures were compared, including Fully Connected Networks (FCNs), FourierNet, Modified FourierNet, and Highway FourierNet. FCNs served as the baseline but struggled to capture sharp gradients due to their low-frequency bias, whereas Fourier-based models used sinusoidal encodings to represent high-frequency variations more effectively. The Modified FourierNet, which employs multiplicative modulation of encoded features, consistently provided the most accurate results and demonstrated superior stability during training.

To understand how architectural choices affect predictive performance, the networks were evaluated at varying depths ranging from two to ten hidden layers. Extremely shallow networks lacked expressive capacity, while very deep networks became harder to train. An eight-layer architecture offered the best balance between representational power and training stability, producing the most accurate velocity predictions along both the horizontal and vertical centerlines when compared to the benchmark dataset of Ghia and Ghia. Training duration was also analyzed by varying the maximum optimization steps, showing that extended training improves accuracy but with diminishing returns; around 8000 steps provided a practical middle ground, while 15,000 steps produced the highest accuracy at the cost of increased computation time.

Optimizer selection played a significant role in model convergence. Among the tested optimizers-SGD, Adam, AdamW, and Ranger-SGD performed the worst due to slow and unstable convergence. Adam and AdamW consistently delivered the best results because of their adaptive learning rate mechanisms, with AdamW slightly outperforming the others in several metrics. Ranger produced competitive results but did not exceed the performance of AdamW. Overall, the combination of the Modified FourierNet architecture, an eight-layer depth, and AdamW optimization provided the most reliable and accurate reconstruction of the cavity flow.

The study demonstrates that Fourier-enhanced PINNs can accurately reproduce the velocity fields, vortical structures, and benchmark centerline profiles of the lid-driven cavity problem without requiring a numerical mesh. These findings highlight the potential of PINNs as flexible surrogates for CFD, capable of capturing the underlying physics directly through PDE-based loss terms. They also suggest promising avenues for future work, including scaling toward turbulent regimes, multi-physics interactions, hybrid PINN-CFD frameworks, and applications in industrial simulation. This research was accepted for presentation at the 12th International and 52nd National Conference on Fluid Mechanics and Fluid Power (FMFP-2025), underscoring its relevance to advances in physics-informed machine learning and computational fluid mechanics.

Extended Research and Current Work

Our research team, under the guidance of Dr. A. M. Lakdawala, successfully completed and presented a study on the application of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) for solving benchmark fluid flow problems, culminating in a research paper accepted at the 12th International and 52nd National Conference on Fluid Mechanics and Fluid Power (FMFP–2025). This work established a strong foundation in physics-constrained machine learning, demonstrating the capability of PINNs to accurately replicate classical CFD solutions without reliance on numerical discretization.

Building on the methodologies, insights, and computational experience gained from this project, we have expanded our research toward a highly impactful interdisciplinary application—using PINNs for biomedical thermal therapy modeling. Our current project focuses on the Physics-Informed Neural Network Simulation of Breast Cancer Treatment via Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA).

In this work, we integrate PINNs with OpenFOAM to simulate heat-induced tumor destruction, aiming to:

  • Model the thermal response of malignant breast tissue
  • Predict tumor behavior during RFA exposure
  • Optimize treatment parameters for more effective and minimally invasive therapy

In recognition of this contribution and its interdisciplinary potential, I have been offered a research-based internship at IIT Bombay under the mentorship of Prof. Atul Sharma, providing an opportunity to further advance this work within a highly specialized academic environment.
