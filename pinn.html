IIT Bombay Research Internship

PINN Based CFD Development for Multiphase Problems Using Level-Set Method

During my major research internship at Indian Institute of Technology Bombay (IIT Bombay), I worked at the Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) Laboratory under the mentorship of Prof. Atul Sharma and guidance of Dr. A. M. Lakdawala. The project focused on the development of a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) framework for multiphase flow problems using the Level-Set Method.

The research explored the integration of Physics-Informed Machine Learning with classical Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) techniques for solving moving-interface multiphase flow problems. The work emphasized interface transport, curvature prediction, surface tension modeling, and hybrid PINN-CFD frameworks for bubble dynamics.

The project followed a structured multi-stage methodology beginning with simplified advection and interface transport problems, progressing toward static bubble validation and finally the implementation of a hybrid PINN framework for the bubble rise problem.

  • Implementation of PINNs for interface tracking using Level-Set methods
  • Comparative study of AD, ND, and Coupled AD-ND (CAN) differentiation strategies
  • Hybrid CFD-PINN framework for bubble rise reconstruction
  • Multiphase flow modeling involving curvature and surface tension effects
  • Validation against CFD-generated data using OpenFOAM-based workflows

Research Highlights

The study demonstrated the capability of hybrid physics-informed learning frameworks to reconstruct velocity and pressure fields for multiphase bubble rise problems while maintaining physical consistency and reducing dependence on full CFD simulations.

The framework combined governing equation constraints with CFD-assisted learning to improve prediction stability and interface accuracy. The work particularly focused on overcoming limitations associated with long-time PINN training instability in interface-dominated flows.

The developed methodology established a strong foundation for future research involving:

  • Hybrid PINN-CFD multiphase solvers
  • AI-assisted interface tracking
  • Physics-informed surrogate modeling
  • High-performance scientific machine learning
  • Advanced bubble dynamics simulations
Research Publication

Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Lid-Driven Cavity Flow

This work investigates the use of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) as mesh-free and data-efficient alternatives to traditional CFD solvers by solving the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations for the classical lid-driven cavity problem. PINNs embed continuity, momentum conservation, and boundary conditions directly into the loss function, allowing accurate flow predictions without numerical discretization or large datasets. The cavity benchmark-simple in geometry yet rich in vortical features provides an ideal test case to evaluate their accuracy.

The study was implemented using NVIDIA’s PhysicsNeMo, enabling symbolic PDE definitions and automatic differentiation for residual computation. Multiple architectures were tested, including Fully Connected Networks (FCNs), FourierNet, Modified FourierNet, and Highway FourierNet. FCNs exhibited low-frequency limitations, while Fourier-based models captured sharp gradients more effectively. The Modified FourierNet consistently achieved the highest stability and accuracy.

Model depth was varied from 2-10 layers, revealing that shallow networks underfit and very deep ones became difficult to train. An 8-layer architecture provided the best balance and reproduced the benchmark centerline velocity profiles of Ghia and Ghia with high fidelity. Training step analysis showed diminishing accuracy gains beyond 8000 steps, with 15,000 steps offering the most refined results at increased computational cost.

Among tested optimizers-SGD, Adam, AdamW, and Ranger: AdamW delivered the most reliable convergence and final accuracy. Overall, combining Modified FourierNet, an 8-layer depth, and AdamW produced the best reconstruction of the lid-driven cavity flow, effectively capturing vortices and velocity fields without mesh-based numerics.

These results demonstrate the capability of Fourier-enhanced PINNs as accurate and flexible surrogates for CFD, with potential for future extensions to turbulent flows, multi-physics coupling, and hybrid PINN-CFD frameworks. This research was accepted for presentation at the 12th International and 52nd National Conference on Fluid Mechanics and Fluid Power (FMFP-2025).
