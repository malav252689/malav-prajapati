Research Paper: Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Lid-Driven Cavity Flow

This work investigates the use of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) as mesh-free and data-efficient alternatives to traditional CFD solvers by solving the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations for the classical lid-driven cavity problem. PINNs embed continuity, momentum conservation, and boundary conditions directly into the loss function, allowing accurate flow predictions without numerical discretization or large datasets. The cavity benchmark-simple in geometry yet rich in vortical features provides an ideal test case to evaluate their accuracy.

The study was implemented using NVIDIA’s PhysicsNeMo, enabling symbolic PDE definitions and automatic differentiation for residual computation. Multiple architectures were tested, including Fully Connected Networks (FCNs), FourierNet, Modified FourierNet, and Highway FourierNet. FCNs exhibited low-frequency limitations, while Fourier-based models captured sharp gradients more effectively. The Modified FourierNet consistently achieved the highest stability and accuracy.

Model depth was varied from 2-10 layers, revealing that shallow networks underfit and very deep ones became difficult to train. An 8-layer architecture provided the best balance and reproduced the benchmark centerline velocity profiles of Ghia and Ghia with high fidelity. Training step analysis showed diminishing accuracy gains beyond 8000 steps, with 15,000 steps offering the most refined results at increased computational cost.

Among tested optimizers-SGD, Adam, AdamW, and Ranger: AdamW delivered the most reliable convergence and final accuracy. Overall, combining Modified FourierNet, an 8-layer depth, and AdamW produced the best reconstruction of the lid-driven cavity flow, effectively capturing vortices and velocity fields without mesh-based numerics.

These results demonstrate the capability of Fourier-enhanced PINNs as accurate and flexible surrogates for CFD, with potential for future extensions to turbulent flows, multi-physics coupling, and hybrid PINN-CFD frameworks. This research was accepted for presentation at the 12th International and 52nd National Conference on Fluid Mechanics and Fluid Power (FMFP-2025).

Extended Research and Current Work

Our research team, under the guidance of Dr. A. M. Lakdawala, successfully completed and presented a study on the application of Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) for solving benchmark fluid flow problems, culminating in a research paper accepted at the 12th International and 52nd National Conference on Fluid Mechanics and Fluid Power (FMFP-2025). This work established a strong foundation in physics-constrained machine learning, demonstrating the capability of PINNs to accurately replicate classical CFD solutions without reliance on numerical discretization.

Building on the methodologies, insights, and computational experience gained from this project, we have expanded our research toward a highly impactful interdisciplinary application, using PINNs for biomedical thermal modeling. Our current project focuses on the Physics-Informed Neural Network Simulation of Breast Cancer Treatment via Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA).

In this work, we integrate PINNs with OpenFOAM to simulate heat-induced tumor destruction, aiming to:

  • Model the thermal response of malignant breast tissue
  • Predict tumor behavior during RFA exposure
  • Optimize treatment parameters for more effective and minimally invasive therapy

In recognition of this contribution and its interdisciplinary potential, I have been offered a research-based internship at IIT Bombay under the mentorship of Prof. Atul Sharma, providing an opportunity to further advance this work within a highly specialized academic environment.
