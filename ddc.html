Drone Design Challenge (DDC) Regular Class

The SAE Drone Development Challenge (DDC) is a national-level competition that challenges students to design, build, and demonstrate efficient unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) capable of carrying payloads within strict dimensional and weight constraints. The Regular Class category emphasizes high payload fractions, versatile design, and innovative engineering solutions. The SAEISS Drone Design Challenge (DDC) is a national UAV design and flight competition that tests engineering skills in aircraft design, performance optimization, and mission execution. In the Regular Class, teams must design and fabricate a conventional fixed-wing UAV that satisfies strict geometric and performance constraints while completing mission-based flight tasks. The event provides an excellent platform for students to apply aerodynamics, structural design, and systems engineering principles in a practical and competitive environment.

The competition imposes clear rules and design criteria, including limits on wingspan (typically =< 72 in), total aircraft dimensions (within a 150 in envelope), and an empty weight under 4 kg. The aircraft must also integrate a designated 4" × 4" × 10" payload bay, support secure payload carriage, and demonstrate a reliable airdrop mechanism for a 500 g payload.

Scoring is based on design evaluation, technical inspection, mission performance, and flight safety. Core missions usually involve takeoff within a restricted distance, waypoint navigation, payload transport, and precise airdrop execution. Teams must justify their design through aerodynamic reasoning, structural analysis, propulsion matching, and fabrication quality.

Overall, the Regular Class serves as a practical engineering platform where students apply aerodynamics, structural mechanics, control, and manufacturing knowledge to build efficient, competition-ready UAV systems within real-world constraints.

DDC-Regular 2024 – Design Philosophy and Innovation

In the 2024 edition, Team Arrow focused on developing a high payload fraction UAV that balanced lightweight construction with structural strength and aerodynamic efficiency. As the Design Lead, I guided the design process toward achieving maximum lift and stability while minimizing the empty weight of the aircraft. The team adopted a trailing edge tapered wing configration with a 72-inch span, with reduced wood usage on the exterior portion of the wing and replacing it with monocote film, thereby enhancing aerodynamic smoothness. A three-walled fuselage made of aeroply and aluminium frame provided exceptional strength-to-weight performance, ensuring impact resistance and durability during repeated flight operations. This time, based on the previous experiences team decided to focus on developing a suspension based tri-cycle landing gear, which helped to minimize the landing shocks transmitted to the fuselage and helped to achieve a softer landing for the UAV.

Achievements

  • Overall 4th Place – DDC Regular 2024
  • 2nd Best Design Report Award

Optimization and Flight Performance

Through iterative testing and refinement, the UAV’s empty weight was reduced from 3950 grams to under 3650 grams, achieving a remarkable payload fraction of 0.6. The simplified fuselage design made the UAV easier to manufacture, repair, and optimize between flights, nearly doubling the number of successful flight tests. A dedicated pilot training program ensured precision control, enabling consistent performance throughout testing and competition. The 2024 project reflected the team’s evolution toward a more data-driven, performance-oriented development cycle, marked by continuous improvement and engineering discipline.

DDC-Regular 2023 – Stability and Structural Efficiency

The 2023 UAV focused on achieving high stability and efficient aerodynamic performance, with a strong emphasis on flight control and durability. The aircraft featured a H-tail configuration, which substantially improved pitch and yaw stability and made the UAV easier to handle during pitching action and maneuvers. A circular semi-monocoque fuselage offered a lightweight yet impact-resistant structure, providing ample internal space for electronics and payload integration. To improve landing resilience, foam wheels were used added cushioning at the time of landing, effectively absorbing shocks and protecting critical components.

Achievements

  • Best CFD Award – DDC Regular Class 2023
  • 2nd Best Design Report Award

Optimization and Flight Performance

Throughout the 2023 development phase, the team continuously refined the UAV through experimentation and testing. The wing mounting system evolved from a slotted lock to a more reliable wing nut assembly, improving pre-flight efficiency and robustness. The use of CFRP plates in place of aluminum pipes reduced overall weight of the landing gear while maintaining structural integrity. By optimizing aerodynamics and streamlining assembly, the team achieved significant gains in stability, performance, and operational reliability. These improvements laid the groundwork for the advanced lightweight strategies and design philosophies implemented in the 2024 UAV.
